drive-widget · v1 · the technical sibling of free-disk-space-widget

A drive menu that knows which cable it's on

free-disk-space-widget answers one question: how much room is left. This design is for everything else about the drives. One row per physical drive, not per volume. The drive you pick becomes one larger card, the same anatomy as the Wi‑Fi widget's connected network: live read/write and temperature, SMART health, the bus and bridge chip it actually negotiated, and the volumes and formats on it. The Right now scenario uses what this Mac reported on 23 Sep 2026. The other scenarios are illustrative.

Drives, not volumes

Disk Utility and the Finder list volumes. That's why an APFS container shows up three times and a 2‑bay enclosure looks like two unrelated disks. Here, a row is a physical device: a whole IOMedia with a real transport underneath. Synthesized APFS disks are folded into the drive that stores them, and Preboot, VM, Recovery and Update are hidden unless you turn on Show System Volumes.

Click any row to open it as the card. The card starts on whatever needs attention: the fullest boot disk, a SMART warning or a slow link. When nothing does, it starts on the startup disk.

Two LEDs, like the front of a tower

The Smart style's glyph has two dots next to it: green for read, red for write. They light for any interval where any drive moved bytes. That tells you something is thrashing the disk without spending menu bar width on numbers. The counters are the kernel's own per-drive totals, so reading them costs nothing, even once a second.

Hover to copy

BSD name, serial, mount point and volume UUID copy on click, like IP and MAC in the Wi‑Fi card. The Speed row is the one action on the card: it runs iospeed against the first writable volume, and only when you click it.

P0 edge states

Missing evidence stays visible

No SMART path

Card: the Health group shows a single row: UNAVAILABLE and the reason, for example ASMedia ASM1352R bridge, macOS has no passthrough. The temperature column shows a dim dash, never 0 °C.

Never show a green tick for a drive that simply can't answer. Unknown health is grey, not good. That is three of the four drives on this Mac today.

Empty enclosure

A USB bridge with nothing behind it still enumerates as a block device with no media. That's the Realtek RTL9210B on your USB 2.0 hub right now. It gets a dimmed row that says No disk, with its link speed, because "why is my SSD not showing up" usually starts here: a bad M.2 seat, or a drive that didn't spin up.

Read-only or foreign format

NTFS mounts read-only, and ext4 or an unknown partition type doesn't mount at all. The row still appears with its partition type and a READ-ONLY or NOT MOUNTED tag. The Speed row turns off, because iospeed has to write a test file.

Locked volume

An encrypted APFS volume that hasn't been unlocked shows its size and a LOCKED tag in the Volumes group, plus an Unlock… action that hands off to the system password prompt. The widget never stores or sees the passphrase.

Why the Bus group exists

The slowest link sets the ceiling

A drive runs at the speed of the narrowest thing between it and the Mac: the drive itself, the bridge chip in the enclosure, any hub, and the port. The Bus group names all of them and the speed they negotiated. The widget flags a drive when the link is well below what the drive could do. The scale below is practical sequential MB/s on a log axis. Solid markers are your drives today; the dotted one is what the SN740 does in a better enclosure.

The two 16 TB drives are limited by their platters, not the cable. Each manages about 270 MB/s. Both together still fit inside the ASM1352R's 10 Gb/s uplink, so sharing the bridge isn't a bottleneck. Sharing a power supply is the real risk (see below).

The SN740 is capped at about a fifth of its speed. It's a PCIe 4 NVMe drive that can do about 5 GB/s, in a 10 Gb/s Realtek bridge. That's fine for a scratch disk, but it's why the card shows drive ~5 000 · link ~1 000 rather than just one number.

What's plugged in

I probed this Mac, read-only

Using diskutil, ioreg, system_profiler, smartctl and iostat, all read-only. Nothing was mounted, unmounted or written. This is the tree the widget has to draw, and it has more going on than the Finder sidebar shows.

Works

Internal SSD: full NVMe SMART

smartctl reads the NVMe health log with no root and no driver: PASSED, 35 °C, 0% used, 100% spare, 20.4 TB written, 15.6 TB read, 435 power-on hours, 274 power cycles, 35 unsafe shutdowns, 0 media errors. The widget can get all of this directly from the NVMeSMARTLib IOKit plug-in, without shelling out.

Blocked on macOS

USB drives: no SMART at all

All three external disks return Operation not supported by device, and diskutil reports SMART Status: Not Supported. The bridges could pass SMART through (smartctl has -d sntrealtek and -d sat for exactly these chips), but Apple's USB mass-storage driver doesn't expose passthrough. The only fix is a third-party DriverKit extension such as the open-source SAT SMART Driver. The widget should detect it when it's installed and never require it.

Worth a warning

Tower Backup lives next to Tower

Both 16 TB drives sit behind a single ASMedia ASM1352R bridge (one USB device, LUN 0 and LUN 1), on the same hub, and in the same enclosure. The backup shares a power supply, a bridge chip and a cable with the drive it backs up. The widget can see that from the IORegistry tree and says so once, in the Bus group, as Shares enclosure with Tower. It's not an alarm, just a fact you wouldn't otherwise see.

Two full disks

Capacity, today

Macintosh HD: 10.4 GB free of 494 GB (98%). DiscPro2TB: 7.3 GB free of 2 TB (exFAT, 128 KB clusters, so small files waste space). Tower 84%, Tower Backup 91%. Tower is case-sensitive APFS, and the Volumes group labels it that way, because some apps and backup tools stumble on it.

Odd but harmless

An enclosure on the slow side of the hub

A second Realtek bridge (RTL9210B) is plugged into the hub's USB 2.0 half at 480 Mb/s, with no media behind it. If a drive goes into it, it will run at about 40 MB/s. That's the USB 2 link scenario, and exactly what the Bus group is for.

Live counters

I/O since boot

iostat totals since boot: disk0 has moved 1.25 TB, Tower 9.8 GB, Tower Backup 0.8 GB and DiscPro 15 MB. The same per-drive byte counters drive the LEDs, the Read/Write numbers and the sparkline. One sample showed disk0 at 3.2 MB/s and everything else idle.

Build notes

What it reads, and from where

IOKit, through objc2

Walk IOMedia where Whole = true and go up to the IOBlockStorageDevice: Device Characteristics (vendor, product, revision, serial, medium type) and Protocol Characteristics (Physical Interconnect: USB, Apple Fabric, PCI-Express or Thunderbolt, plus location). Further up, IOUSBHostDevice gives the negotiated speed and the bridge's USB vendor and product ID, which tell you the chip: 0bda:9210 is Realtek, 174c:55aa is ASMedia. It's the same crate stack as battery-widget.

Throughput from counters

IOBlockStorageDriver publishes a Statistics dictionary with bytes and operations read and written, plus total read and write time. The widget samples it once a second while the menu is open and every 2 s for the LEDs. It works out MB/s, IOPS and average latency from the differences. This is the same source iostat uses. No benchmark, no fs_usage, no root.

DiskArbitration for mounts and eject

DARegisterDiskAppearedCallback and DiskDisappeared keep the list live with no polling. Eject goes through DADiskEject for the whole drive, so an enclosure with two volumes ejects both. When eject fails, the dissenter gives a status, and a proc_listpidspath lookup on the mount point names the processes holding it open. A disappear event without a prior unmount is recorded as an unsafe removal.

SMART, with an honest fallback

NVMe: IONVMeSMARTUserClient through NVMeSMARTLib, the same path smartctl uses. SATA inside a Mac Pro or a Thunderbolt dock: IOATASMARTUserClient. USB: only when a SAT passthrough DriverKit extension is loaded, detected by its IORegistry class. Health isn't just the raw values. The widget keeps one reading a day per serial in ~/.local/state/drive-widget/ and warns on change: a sector count that is rising, spare that is dropping, or unsafe shutdowns that jumped.

Speed test is iospeed

src/iospeed already writes a test file with the cache bypassed and reports sequential read and write. The widget runs it with -size 1G in the background and parses the plain output. It is disabled when the volume has less than 2 GB free, is read-only, or is on battery below 20%. Results are saved per volume, with a date, and shown in the Speed row.

Relationship to free-disk-space-widget

Keep both. The free-space widget is the always-on number, and this is the drawer you open when something is off. They share the capacity tiers: under 10% free is red and under 20% is amber. Icon + Free here is deliberately the same number the sibling shows, so you can run this one alone if you prefer.
