job-monitor · tray icon concepts

Per-job indicators instead of the aggregate cursor + three queue dots: the icon grows one slot per job up to a cap, so the width itself says how deep the queue is. Each concept below is shown at real menu-bar size on light and dark bars, plus a 4× zoom. All neutral states stay template-tintable; red remains reserved for failures.

Ajob ticks

Keep the frame + chevron as the identity mark; drop the cursor and dots. After the frame, every job gets a vertical tick: running is full-height and blinks, queued is a dim half-height tick, failed is red with a dropped dot (an exclamation mark rotated into the lane). Reads like a punch card of the queue.

idle
no jobs
1 running
1 running + 2 queued
2 running + 3 queued
1 failed · 1 running · 1 queued
12 jobs (capped)
offline (monitor, folder gone)
  • Width: BASE 16pt + 3.4pt × min(jobs, 7) → 16–39.8pt. Cap slot becomes a dim + when jobs > 7.
  • Ticks are ordered running-first, then queued, then failed — the eye finds the blink at the left of the lane.
  • Failure is visible per-job, not just as one red chevron: two failures = two red ticks.

Bqueue on screen

The screen frame itself is the container: it widens and the jobs render inside it as blocks, like a terminal showing its own queue. The chevron shrinks to a prompt at the left edge. Running blocks blink solid, queued are dim hollow outlines, failed are red. The nicest conceptual fit — the monitor literally displays the jobs — at the cost of small interior geometry.

  • Frame width: 14pt + 4.2pt × max(0, min(jobs,6) − 1); interior blocks are 2.6×5.4pt on a 4.2pt pitch.
  • Cap at 6 blocks; overflow renders the last block as three tiny dots (ellipsis) inside the frame.
  • Offline keeps today’s tell: dim frame, dim doubled chevron, empty screen.

Ca cursor per job

The straightest extension of the current metaphor: the cursor slot becomes a lane of cursors, one per job. Running is the familiar blinking block, queued is the resting underscore (dimming with position, like the dots do today), failed is the red !. Idle with an empty queue is exactly today’s icon — nothing to relearn.

no jobs
1 running

offline (monitor, folder gone)
  • Width: 12pt + 3.9pt × min(jobs, 6); idle collapses to today’s BASE_WIDTH with the resting underscore.
  • Underscores fade with queue position (0.6 → 0.25), preserving the current “trailing off” read.
  • Overflow past 6: last slot is a dim mid-height + glyph drawn as two strokes.

Dequalizer

Densest option: drop the frame entirely, keep the chevron as the mark, and give every job a thin 2pt bar — running tall with a slow two-frame bob (uses the existing blink clock), queued low and dim, failed red. Because bars are on a 3pt pitch the cap can be generous (10) before the icon gets wide.

no jobs
1 running
1 running + 2 queued
2 running + 3 queued
1 failed · 1 running · 1 queued
12 jobs (capped)
offline (monitor, folder gone)
  • Width: 10pt + 3pt × min(jobs, 10) → at 10 jobs it’s still narrower than concept B at 6.
  • The “bob” alternates running bars between two heights on the blink timer — motion without a blackout frame.
  • Weakest identity when idle (just a chevron + underscore); the frame is what makes the current icon recognisable.

Recommendation: C is the safest win — it inherits every rule the README already teaches (block = running, underscore = waiting, red ! = failed, doubled chevron = monitor, empty slot = offline) and simply pluralises the cursor. B is the most charming if you’re happy to trade a little legibility at 1× for the “terminal showing its queue” conceit — check the 6-job dark-bar strip above before committing. A and D are the fallbacks if you want more density than C allows.
